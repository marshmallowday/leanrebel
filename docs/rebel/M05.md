## Mathematical domain

The canonical theorem uses a two-player `ExecutionProtocol`, finite legal
histories, finite action carriers, an information-local legal fallback policy,
and a finite remaining runner fuel. The abstract information model requires
perfect recall and an observation clock for equilibrium existence. The
full-AOH factory discharges the memory/clock obligations using the original
protocol. Utilities are real-valued and zero-sum. Neither an equilibrium nor
the desired infostate-value identity is assumed as a field of a game oracle.

A `TypeBeliefSlice` holds a compatible conditional joint-history law K_t for
each own root type t. Own probabilities p vary over their entire simplex while
these kernels and the opponent behavioral policy remain fixed. The initial
joint PBS is reconstructed as the mixture of K_t with weights p_t.

This is a conditional-law slice, NOT a claim that arbitrary opponent marginals
determine the joint history law. Reweighting own types can change an opponent
marginal in a correlated game. Consequently the paper's marginal notation
beta_-i is interpreted through compatible fixed kernels, not an unjustified
product-of-marginals reconstruction. Opponent policies range over all legal
behavioral policies, not just pure strategies or on-policy reachable choices.

`PublicRootType` includes every physically realized full AOH in the public
fiber, including types with current probability zero. Positive-mass fibers
use conditioning of the actual joint law. A zero-mass fiber needs a completion;
`publicRootOffPath` chooses a physically compatible history. This completion
is explicit and not asserted to be uniquely determined by the current PBS.
Other compatible fixed completions are supported by the abstract slice API.
No zero-mass type is silently removed or assigned zero continuation value.

## Extension and quantifiers: R3 disposition

Write a_sigma(t) for the actual typewise best-response value against a fixed
opponent and H(w) = inf_sigma sum_t w_t a_sigma(t). Finite-history payoff bounds
make this real-valued even for signed weights. On the simplex it is the
canonical PBS equilibrium value. For a fixed base belief p, define

    F_p(w) = H(w) + H(p) * (1 - sum_t w_t).

This is concave on the whole finite-dimensional real vector space and agrees
with H on the entire simplex. Every equilibrium opponent at p supplies

    g_sigma(t) = a_sigma(t) - H(p),
    F_p(w) <= F_p(p) + sum_t g_sigma(t) * (w_t - p_t).

Thus Eq. (2) holds with a genuine global supporting vector for F_p, including
nonsmooth and boundary base beliefs. The chosen extension depends on the BASE
belief, not on which equilibrium opponent is selected there. The implemented
quantifier order is: fix a compatible slice and p; the displayed F_p works for
every equilibrium at p. No single universal extension for all base beliefs is
claimed. This is the adopted existential-extension reading of the main paper.

The supplement instead specifies the degree-zero radial normalization
H(w / sum w). That is a different function. `ValueRadial.value_eq` computes the
canonical minimax value of a finite zero-sum type game; its `radialValue` is
x/(x+y). `radialValue_hasFDerivAt` and `gradient_eq_centered` identify the actual
Frechet derivative at (1/2,1/2), yet `radial_gradient_not_supporting` refutes its
global support even on strictly positive weights. `radialValue_not_concave`
provides a Jensen counterexample. These are game-value theorems, not only the
older rational arithmetic diagnostic. The normalized-extension version of the
supplement is refuted; the repaired extension must not be relabeled as it.

`ValueDifferential` separately proves the nonzero-mass normalization Jacobian,
its simplex specialization, and the centered derivative of each FIXED branch.
It does not differentiate a nonsmooth minimum or infer global concavity from a
chain rule. The derivative of a fixed conditional value with respect to own
weights is zero precisely because the kernels and opponent remain fixed.

## Adversarial and concrete controls

`Examples/ValuePBS.twoStage_joint_theorem1` instantiates the full canonical
construction in the genuine two-stage hidden-type protocol for an arbitrary
joint type law. Its `valueSlice_preserves_joint` preserves correlated and point
mass laws; `valueSlice_correlation_control` retains different canonical payoffs
for correlated and anticorrelated laws having equal marginals.

`Examples/ValueRadial.zero_mass_type_control` has own type probability zero but
conditional best-response value one. Its `corrected_support` contrasts the
repaired extension with the false radial-support assertion.

`Examples/ValueKink.value_eq` derives min(p,1-p) from canonical finite-game
minimax. `value_not_differentiable`, `both_equilibrium_opponents`,
`distinct_equilibrium_opponents`, and `distinct_centered_vectors` prove the
nonsmooth/nonunique behavior. `nonsmooth_support` proves support without an
imaginary derivative. This is an explicit guessing-game control for the
Figure 3 phenomenon, not a claim of literal payoff-scale/sign transcription
of every annotation in the published figure.

`averaged_global_support` uses a finite probability law of optimal opponents.
Those coefficients are nonnegative and sum to one. The stronger arbitrary
linear-combination wording of footnote 8 is refuted by
`ValueKink.arbitrary_linear_combination_not_supporting`; convex averaging is
the corrected statement.
