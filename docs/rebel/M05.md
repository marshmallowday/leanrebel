## Mathematical domain

The canonical theorem uses a two-player `ExecutionProtocol`, finite legal
histories and action carriers, an information-local legal fallback policy, and
finite remaining runner fuel. The focal player is labelled zero. Player labels
are chosen when instantiating the theorem; this entry point does not assert an
additional within-protocol role-renaming equivalence. Utilities are real and
zero-sum. The abstract information model requires perfect recall and an
observation clock; the full-AOH factory discharges these using the original
protocol. Neither equilibrium existence nor the desired infostate-value
identity is assumed as a field of a game/value oracle.

A `TypeBeliefSlice` fixes a compatible conditional joint-history law K_t for
each own root type t. Own probabilities p vary over the entire type simplex,
while K_t and the opponent behavioral policy remain fixed. The original joint
PBS is reconstructed as the mixture of K_t with the weights p_t.

This is a conditional-law slice, NOT a claim that arbitrary opponent marginals
determine a joint law. Reweighting own types can change an opponent marginal
in a correlated game. The paper's beta_-i notation is interpreted through
compatible fixed kernels, not an unjustified product-of-marginals replacement.
Opponent policies range over all legal behavioral policies, not just pure or
on-policy reachable strategies.

`PublicRootType` includes every physically realized full AOH in the public
fiber, including types with current probability zero. Positive-mass fibers
use the actual joint law's conditional distribution. A zero-mass fiber needs
a completion; `publicRootOffPath` chooses a physically compatible history.
This is not asserted to be uniquely determined by the current PBS. Other
compatible fixed completions are supported by the abstract slice API. No
zero-mass type is silently removed or assigned zero continuation value.

4. `BeliefExistence` transfers a finite-plan mixed equilibrium to the canonical
   PBS behavioral game. `exists_nash_with_value` supplies an actual equilibrium;
   `value_eq_equilibriumPayoff` identifies the scalar payoff of EVERY equilibrium.

5. `branch_isLeast_of_nash` proves attained minimax over all behavioral opponents.
   `branch_eq_value_iff_nashOpponent` proves the converse: exactly minimizing

   an existing equilibrium row, not with an arbitrary best response.
   `value_concaveOn_simplex` supplies Lemma 2's concavity.
6. `theorem1_canonicalPBS` connects any given canonical equilibrium, its Eq. (1)

## Extension and quantifiers: R3 disposition

Let a_sigma(t) be the actual typewise best-response value against a fixed
opponent. Define H(w) = inf_sigma sum_t w_t a_sigma(t). Finite-history payoff
bounds make it real-valued even for signed weights. On the simplex H is the
canonical PBS equilibrium value. At a fixed base belief p, define

```text
F_p(w) = H(w) + H(p) * (1 - sum_t w_t).
g_sigma(t) = a_sigma(t) - H(p).
```

F_p is concave on the whole finite-dimensional real vector space and agrees
with H on the entire simplex. Every equilibrium opponent at p supplies

```text
F_p(w) <= F_p(p) + sum_t g_sigma(t) * (w_t - p_t).
```

Eq. (2) therefore holds with a genuine global supporting vector, including
nonsmooth and boundary base points. The extension depends on the BASE belief,
not on which equilibrium opponent is selected there. The quantifier order is:
fix a compatible slice and p; the displayed F_p works for every equilibrium
at p. No single universal extension independent of p is claimed. This is the
adopted existential-extension reading of the main paper, with the fixed-kernel
interpretation and finite game domain made explicit.

The supplement instead specifies the degree-zero radial normalization
H(w / sum w). That is a different function. `ValueRadial.value_eq` computes
the actual canonical minimax value of an indicator type game; its `radialValue`
is x/(x+y). `radialValue_hasFDerivAt` and `gradient_eq_centered` identify the
actual Frechet derivative at (1/2,1/2), yet `radial_gradient_not_supporting`
refutes global support even on strictly positive weights.
`radialValue_not_concave` supplies a Jensen counterexample. These are game-value
theorems, not only the older M01 rational arithmetic diagnostic.

`normal_shift_annihilates_tangent` proves that (1/4,1/4) is normal to the
mass-one simplex. `radialValue_changes_along_simplex_normal` then computes a
change from 1/4 to 1/3 along that shift. Constancy on rays is not constancy
perpendicular to the simplex. Thus the supplement's concavity rationale and
global-support interpretation are refuted. The repaired extension must never
be relabelled as the supplement's normalized extension.

## Differential calculation and boundary discipline

`ValueDifferential` proves the nonzero-mass normalization Jacobian, its simplex
specialization and the centered derivative of each FIXED branch. The zero
own-weight derivative of a conditional value is justified because its kernel
and opponent are fixed. The proof does not differentiate a nonsmooth minimum
or infer global concavity merely from a chain rule.

Here normalization is by the signed coordinate SUM. On nonnegative vectors
this agrees with normalization by the l1 norm. On general signed vectors those
are different operations. Derivatives at simplex boundary points use the
specified nonzero-sum extension, not a false differentiability claim for the
signed l1 norm. Zero total mass is excluded from differential statements.
Global support for F_p does not require either interiority or differentiability.

## Adversarial examples and convex averaging

`Examples/ValueRadial.zero_mass_type_control` has own type probability zero
but conditional best-response value one. `corrected_support` contrasts the
repaired extension with the false radial-support assertion on the same game.

The explicit hidden-coin game in `Examples/ValueGeometry` gives informed-player
payoff -1 for a correct opponent guess and +1 otherwise. `coin_value` computes
its canonical minimax value and `coinSimplexValue_eq` derives -abs(2p-1).
`coinSimplexValue_not_differentiable`, `coin_nonunique_opponents` and
`coin_midpoint_support` prove the nonsmooth, nonunique-equilibrium phenomenon
of Figure 3. This does not claim a new transcription of every visual annotation
in the published figure.

`Examples/ValueKink` is a separate payoff-scaled/oriented control whose value
is min(p,1-p). It proves actual equilibrium membership for two distinct pure
opponents, distinct centered vectors, nonsmoothness and support without an
imaginary derivative. `arbitrary_linear_combination_not_supporting` refutes
footnote 8's unrestricted linear-combination wording.

`averaged_global_support` uses a finite probability law of optimal opponents
at a common base. Its coefficients are nonnegative and sum to one. This is the
correct convex-averaging statement, not arbitrary linear closure.
