## 1. Public theorem and precise target class

`GameTheory.ReBeL.cfrAveragedProfile_isNash`, in
`GameTheory/Analysis/ReBeL/CFRNash.lean`, constructs and proves the guarantee for
the actual simultaneous full-game CFR sequence. Its remaining inputs are:

* A canonical two-player `ExecutionProtocol` / `InformationModel`, finite legal
  histories and legal menus, an information-local observation clock, perfect
  recall, and a complete legal fallback policy.
* Canonical zero-sum history payoffs, explicit nonnegative absolute payoff
  bounds, a fixed finite horizon, and a positive iteration count.

The conclusion is the existing `IsNash` predicate with `euPreferenceWithin` on
`M.toBehavioralGameForm horizon`. It quantifies over **every complete legal
information-local behavioral deviation**. It is not Nash in a hand-selected
finite strategy subset, not a correlated-equilibrium statement, and not a
claim about the last iterate. Full legal histories, not current-policy support,
define the finite domain. Off-policy histories and their deviations remain.
The clock is an explicit structural hypothesis, not an assumption that an
arbitrary imperfect-recall or nonsynchronous encoding satisfies the theorem.
For a full-game application the horizon covers the game's proved termination
bound (three for the concrete example). At a shorter horizon the statement is
about that fixed-horizon canonical game, not an asserted full-game equivalence
or a theorem for changing learned leaf values.

For player `i`, let `S_i` be the actual list of scheduled decision sites and
let `C_I = sqrt(card Choice_I) * (2 * card InformationHistory_I * B_i)`.
The exact implemented cumulative bound is
`R_i(T) = sum_{I in S_i} 2 * C_I * sqrt(T)`.
The theorem's epsilon is `R_0(T)/T + R_1(T)/T`. Constants depend on the fixed
finite game, not on the chosen deviation or T. Thus the bound has the usual
inverse-square-root rate for positive T; it is deliberately conservative, not
the sharper numerical constant in the original CFR paper.

Neither local payoff realization, a local regret bound, a root regret
certificate, nor an optimizer oracle is a premise of this final theorem.
Intermediate lemmas expose such interfaces, but the constructed solver supplies
them from game semantics before invoking the public guarantee.

## 3. Actual best responses and exact equilibrium value

`FiniteSites.exists_pure_bestResponse` uses the full legal information cover
and the predraw mixture equality to obtain an attaining finite pure plan
against arbitrary fixed legal behavioral opponents. Every behavioral
alternative is an expectation over those plans. The total legal fallback
rules out an empty strategy domain and completes off-path choices. This is an
existence proof, not only an `IsGreatest` characterization or a supplied oracle.

`nash_value_eq` proves equality of the value of two **given** exact equilibria
in any canonical two-player zero-sum game form. `behavioralNash_value_eq`
specializes it directly to the original behavioral runner, without adding a
second randomization layer. The regression game has two distinct exact
equilibria with common nonzero value one and a strictly inferior row. A second,
non-zero-sum coordination game has two exact equilibria with different values;
it proves why the zero-sum premise cannot simply be dropped.

These results do not assert equilibrium existence, minimax, or a defined PBS
value at every belief. Those are M05 obligations and remain pending.

## 4. Executable rational solver and concrete refinement

`GameTheory/ReBeL/Rational/FullGame.lean` is an executable rational reference
solver over a finite `HistoryTable`. The table supplies primitive histories,
legal choices, information, transitions, chance factors, own action paths and
payoffs, not a stored regret sequence or the desired correctness conclusion.
The analytic refinement modules connect the primitives, canonical execution,
reach, local regrets, matching, simultaneous iteration and own-reach averaging.

The concrete two-stage hidden-type instance discharges the table certificates,
codec, menu and information-fiber correctness, perfect recall, clock, zero sum,
and payoff bound. In
`GameTheory.ReBeL.Rational.HiddenTypes.Canonical.solve_isNash`, **only a positive
natural iteration count remains**. The actual rational `solve T` output
realizes the same canonical behavioral profile that satisfies the generic
finite-T Nash guarantee. `solve_value_correct` also connects that output to its
canonical root value. This is a Lean proof for every positive T, not an
inference from the first few numerical runs.

The instance retains 85 legal history prefixes and ten active information
sites per player: two first decisions and eight second decisions retaining own
private type, earlier action and public information. There are 1,024 pure
information-local policies per player. It has genuine chance, hidden
information, nonzero payoffs, two decision stages and legal zero-reach choices.

## 5. Fixed execution conventions and independent checks

The initial regret table is zero. All local updates for an iteration read the
same previous-round profile; they are not alternating player updates. A
nonpositive positive-regret total selects the explicit legal fallback. The
abstract API permits the documented fallback, and the concrete reference uses
a specified pure fallback rather than claiming the source paper's uniform tie
rule. `cfrPlay_zero` and the concrete `solve_zero` fix initial behavior.

For T>0 the returned average includes **rounds 0 through T-1**, not round T.
At T=1 it is therefore the initial profile. At T=0 there is no probability law
on `Fin 0` and no division-based convergence claim; the executable result is
the legal fallback. Zero own-reach denominator also selects that fallback,
while the realization theorem still covers the resulting complete policy.

`check_rational_runtime.py` actually runs Lean, parses all emitted probabilities,
and compares them with a separately written Python `Fraction` implementation.
Its independent normal-form terminal enumerator then evaluates each of the
1,024 pure responses per player against the **Lean-produced** profile.

| T | Compared probabilities | Root value for player 0 | Best-response values (players 0,1) | Deviation gains | NashConv |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---:|
| 0 | 40 | 1 | (1,1) | (0,2) | 2 |
| 1 | 40 | 1 | (1,1) | (0,2) | 2 |
| 2 | 40 | 0 | (0,1) | (0,1) | 1 |

These are regression observations, not claims that T=2 is an exact equilibrium.
Other fixtures reject incorrect arithmetic averages, shared seeds, alternating
updates and invalid iteration counts; they exercise zero and negative regret
fallbacks and nonzero counterfactual effects at zero policy reach. Runtime
checking is recorded as `empirical_documented`, separately from the kernel
refinement theorem.

plugin. Compilation and executable Lean testing ran in GitHub Actions. Local
work was offline inspection and bookkeeping of plugin-downloaded source and
validation artifacts; no local Git or direct GitHub connection was used.

not the entire compiler/runtime/OS/hardware stack and not the adequacy of every
modeling decision. The source review above supplies that separate obligation.

removed after use. The real validation and exact-source snapshot workflows
remain. Reproduction commands are the normal commands in README plus
