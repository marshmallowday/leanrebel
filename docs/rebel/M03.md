## Assumptions and semantic boundaries

### Joint reference law

`GameTheory.ReBeL.PublicBelief` carries a canonical `FinDist E.History` and a
proof that every supported history has its indexed full public trace. It does
not identify a law with the product of its player marginals. Canonical
histories retain legal actions, realized chance transitions, initial signals
and the complete subsequent action-observation history. Distinct histories
that merge into one world state remain distinct.

`ReachWeights` is a separate nonnegative, unnormalized type. A finite carrier
is required for its total-mass operations, and a strictly positive total is
required for normalization. Zero total returns `none`. The reference PBS itself
only needs a finite-support law, not a global finite-history instance.

### Execution, conditioning and zero reach

Prediction uses the existing information-local behavioral runner, including
simultaneous legal joint actions and the actual chance transition law. It does
not replace chance by an extra strategic player. Public observations are then
sampled and conditioned on the predicted joint law.

A conditional PBS requires an actual support witness. Impossible public
observations return `none`; no uniform or prior fallback is called a posterior.
The canonical total `condOnFibre` is used only at observations actually sampled
from its marginal, and `atObservation_law` proves agreement there.

The proofs first show that public disintegration reconstructs the law and
commutes with every continuation kernel, then show that each update and any
finite number of updates agree with canonical execution. Expected reward and
future-value consequences are derived from the law equalities. Fuel, public
trace length and termination are not identified: a live zero-fuel outcome is
a depth cut, not a terminal state.

### Local policy domain and deviations

The full-memory interface is `fullInformation M`. A realizable information
state is one obtained from any canonical history, not only from a history with
positive reach under a reference profile. Restriction therefore retains every
legal off-policy deviation site.

Extension to syntactically valid but unrealizable information states takes an
explicit legal fallback. Terminal/unrealized menus are not assumed nonempty
from the protocol's nonterminal progress property. The characterization
`exists_profile_iff_nonempty_choices` identifies the exact total-policy
nonemptiness requirement; the concrete game examples construct full legal
policies, so their applications do not rely on an empty strategy space.

A public prescription is indexed by the original public history and contains
a legal action distribution for each compatible realizable private AOH. The
referee samples the component for the actual private AOH; the public selector
does not inspect it. `policyEquiv` is a proved equivalence of the legal local
strategy carriers. Coordinatewise encode/decode maps commute with canonical
`Profile.update` and cannot inspect another player's private strategy.

The strategic game runs actual Bayesian updates. Outcome preservation is
proved first, then expected rewards, then matching unilateral deviations in
both directions, and only then both Nash correspondences. These theorems use
finite players, decidable player equality where updates require it, an
explicit legal fallback, a fixed starting joint PBS, finite fuel, and arbitrary
real history utilities. No equilibrium or best-response oracle is assumed.

This proves correspondence for original-public-history-indexed prescriptions.
It does not establish strategic equivalence for an enlarged game in which
players freely condition on extra announcements of opponents' complete plans.
Those announcements would change the information and deviation domains. The
associated source rows remain `qualified`, not unconditionally `verified`.

### Compact representations

The compact decoder retains fixed joint chance/compatibility weights and
multiplies them by one own-reach factor per player. Each own-reach factor is
computed from that player's AOH and own policy alone. These are reach factors,
not arbitrary marginal posterior probabilities.

`historyReach_eq_chance_mul_own` is derived from the canonical runner. For a
public trace of length `depth + 1`, `compactWeights_eq_reference` identifies
the decoded weights with the public restriction of the depth-indexed canonical
law. With a finite history carrier and positive observation mass,
`compact_normalize_eq_condition` proves exact equality with the joint PBS.
The unnormalized equality does not need finite histories; normalization does.

The fixed chance factor retains correlations and excludes impossible joint
states. `incompatible_prob_zero` proves that normalization cannot create a
forbidden pair. The private vectors alone cannot decode arbitrary joint PBSs;
the same-game payoff counterexample below explicitly shows why.

The dimension statements count variable local vector entries. The fixed chance
factor, public-history key and its decoding cost remain additional data. Two
labelled D-die vectors have `2 * F^D` entries; a joint tuple table has
`(F^D)^2`. The one-card example has 104 local entries and 156 action-probability
slots for one 52-type/3-action prescription. These are not universal total
memory or runtime complexity bounds, nor C++ refinement theorems.

### Fixed-prior joint statistic

`tilt_reconstruct` proves sufficiency for a specified fixed-prior likelihood
family. The likelihood depends only on the joint observation tuple, and the
conditional hidden-history kernel is fixed before choosing that likelihood.
Mapping to the joint statistic and reconstructing with the fixed kernel
recovers the posterior exactly. The proof does not assume the desired result
as a representation certificate or choose a posterior-dependent decoder.

`tilt_reconstruct_continuation` preserves every continuation kernel, and
`tilt_joint_statistic_injective` identifies equal posteriors from equal joint
statistic laws in that family. `joint_reconstruct` applies this result to the
compatibility-aware product of local reaches. Individual marginals are not
substituted for the joint statistic. The relevant external sufficient-statistic
claims are proved directly in this finite scope, not imported as axioms or
identified with an entire decentralized-POMDP theory.

### Fully public information and compression

A PBS is pure when its full public trace identifies its canonical history.
Private information that happens to identify a history is insufficient for
this public-belief conclusion. The fully revealing dice control proves the
public injectivity premise and applies it both to supported beliefs and to
actual Bayes conditioning.

Compression of public history requires a proved lossless or sufficient key.
The two public dice are proved lossless on actual post-draw dice histories.
This does not authorize replacing arbitrary histories by current world states,
especially when M02's merging-history example applies, and is not a completed
poker encoding or identity with AlphaZero.

## Adversarial examples on canonical games

`Examples/PublicBelief.lean` constructs two different joint type laws as beliefs
on the same fixed `HiddenTypes.fullPrior` game and the same public-history
fiber. Their complete private-AOH marginals agree for both players, yet the
same legal two-stage continuation policy has cumulative expected reward 2
under one and 0 under the other. The iterative Bayesian referee has the same
computed values. This uses actual history execution, not merely a hand-written
expectation on an unrelated tuple type.

Those two arbitrary supported beliefs are not claimed to be two reachable
posteriors at the same pre-action root under an unchanged uniform prior. They
refute unrestricted marginal decoding. The separate compact theorem concerns
the narrower fixed-chance, policy-induced family.

The same file checks an impossible public observation, constructs a nonempty
full behavioral profile for the all-policy Nash equivalence, preserves an
arbitrary replacement at a legal zero-reach information state, and distinguishes
its zero-fuel cut from termination. The fully revealing dice control proves
public-history injectivity, pure PBS degeneration and pure Bayes posteriors.

`Examples/CompactCards.lean` excludes duplicate cards in every finite deck under
any local reach factors. A two-card update uses action likelihood 1 versus
1/2, obtaining exact posterior probabilities 2/3 and 1/3 and zero for both
forbidden diagonals. A separate zero-mass example returns `none`.

`Examples/BayesObservation.lean` starts with three actual dice histories of
masses 1/2, 1/4 and 1/4. The public observation retains mass 3/4. Its normalized
posterior is 2/3, 1/3 and 0, neither the unchanged prior nor a uniform law on the
remaining histories. The positive-support witness, excluded history and
post-draw public-key equivalence are all proved.

`Examples/BeliefSearch.lean` treats Figure 1b as the naive perfect-information-
style zero-leaf truncation it illustrates. All mixtures tie under that
truncation, although pure rock loses to paper in the original modified-RPS
game while the displayed `(2/5,2/5,1/5)` mixture has value zero against every
response. The frozen inventory described the figure as a public-belief
example; that row is retained and its interpretation corrected in qualified
evidence. This is neither a refutation of the paper nor a claim that search
can never help.
